# Scope Verification: Nigeria Tax Act 2025

Purpose: test the premise behind D-01 (that the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 is in force from
1 January 2026 and repealed the Companies Income Tax Act and the Value Added Tax Act) against
primary sources before any document is acquired.

- Verification date (all sources accessed): 2026-10-18
- Primary sources: the Federal Government Official Gazette, the National Assembly, the Nigeria
  Revenue Service (formerly FIRS), federal ministries and other government bodies.
- Secondary sources (news reports, law-firm and Big Four commentary) were used only to locate
  primary sources or to report events with no primary record online. They are labelled
  **[secondary]** below and are never relied on for the text of the law.

## Verdict on D-01

**The premise is confirmed, with one material qualification.**

1. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (Act No. 7 of 2025) received assent on 26 June 2025 and commences
   on 1 January 2026 (findings F-1 to F-3).
2. It repeals the Companies Income Tax Act, the Value Added Tax Act, the Personal Income Tax
   Act, the Capital Gains Tax Act, the Petroleum Profits Tax Act, the Stamp Duties Act and
   others (F-4). Liabilities for periods before 1 January 2026 stay under the repealed Acts
   (F-5).
3. No amending Act, commencement order or regulation changing the company income tax or VAT
   provisions was found up to 2026-10-18 (F-6). This search has a limit: there is no
   searchable online index of the Federal Gazette, so an instrument published only in print
   could have been missed.
4. **Qualification:** two official-source texts of the Act are in circulation, and they
   differ in provisions that are in scope for v1 (F-7). The gazette published by the Federal
   Government Printer and the copy issued by the National Assembly in January 2026 differ on
   the small-company threshold, on the power to cut the company rate to 25%, and on section
   numbering from section 89 onward. The National Assembly ordered re-gazetting in December
   2025. No re-gazetted text had been found as of 2026-10-18. The Nigeria Tax Administration
   Act 2025 has the same problem, and there are also two different gazette prints of it.

D-01 stands: v1 is built on the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, not on the repealed statutes. C-03
records the one assumption that did not hold: that there was a single official text. Which
text the corpus treats as authoritative was decided as **D-09 (approved 2026-10-18)**: cite
the gazette and keep the National Assembly text as a pinned comparison. See the
[v1 corpus definition](v1_corpus_definition.md).

There is no Nigeria Revenue Service copy of the Act. When checked by hand on 2026-10-18, the
Service's "Nigeria Tax Act, 2025" page (Resources, Tax Laws; last updated January 2026) had
only a written overview and no PDF of the text.

### Differences checked directly in both texts (in-scope provisions)

| Provision | Gazette text | National Assembly text |
|---|---|---|
| "Small company" definition (general interpretation section) | A **company** with gross turnover of **₦50,000,000** or less a year and fixed assets of no more than ₦250,000,000; **businesses providing professional services are excluded** | A **business** with gross turnover of **₦100,000,000** or less a year and fixed assets of no more than ₦250,000,000; no professional-services exclusion |
| Section 56, company rate | 0% small company, 30% other companies | Same, **plus** a proviso allowing reduction to 25% by Presidential order |
| Section 4(1)(j), chargeable income | Gains from "digital or virtual assets" | Gains from "digital assets" |
| Section numbering | Has an extra section 89 ("Petroleum royalty"). Sections 89 onward are one higher than in the other text | Sections 1 to 88 match the gazette; from 89 onward one lower |
| Examples of numbering offset | VAT rate section 148; repeals section 196; interpretation section 202; citation section 203 | VAT rate section 147; repeals section 195; interpretation section 201; citation section 202 |
| Repeals | Includes Taxes and Levies (Approved List for Collection) Act | Does not |
| Name of the Service in definitions | "Nigeria Revenue Service" | "Nigerian Revenue Service" |

Out-of-scope differences were also seen, for example the minimum decommissioning-fund deposit
(30% against 15%). They were not catalogued.

The Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 shows the same pattern, and it also has **two gazette
prints**. All three texts number their sections 1 to 148.

| | Gombe State IRS gazette copy (created July 2025) | NRS gazette copy (uploaded December 2025) | National Assembly copy (document 11250) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commencement under the long title | "[26th June, 2025]" | "[1st Day of January, 2026]" | "[26th June, 2025]" |
| Section 41, appeal from the Tax Appeal Tribunal | 20% of the disputed amount must be paid as security before appeal to the High Court | No deposit clause | No deposit clause |
| Section 29, reporting thresholds | ₦25m (individuals) and ₦100m (bodies corporate) a month | ₦25m (OCR text, partly read) | ₦50m and ₦250m |
| Text layer | Text-based | **Scan with a poor OCR layer**, 27.7 MB | Text-based |

This supports the [secondary] report that the gazettes were reissued in September 2025 after
errors were found. The NRS copy reads as the corrected print. So the Lagos State IRS copy of
the Nigeria Tax Act, created in July 2025, may also be the earlier print. That is logged as
DQ-03. The NRS publishes no copy of the Nigeria Tax Act that could settle it. The Gombe and NRS copies
differ in substance, so each was pinned separately by hash.
